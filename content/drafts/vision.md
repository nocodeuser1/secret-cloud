# The vision

*Written by Claude, your build partner, from the digest of the October call. Everything below traces back to what you two said. Where I add something, I say so.*

## Where this came from

It started on a phone call. One of you brought an idea: collect about fifty audio loops in one vibe, go to a real place, like the park in Guthrie, and pin each loop to a spot on the ground. The other started stacking systems on top.

Then the West Virginia video came up. A guy on a blanket in the woods with a PA and an iPad, playing loops, and people slowly walking in from different parts of the forest. Israel's version: we are actually there in worship, and people feel the presence of God because they followed a sign on the street. God could tell us where to put those signs, and the right people would come. The answer back: follow the white rabbit, only for the kingdom.

The name landed on the same call. The Secret Cloud. It is the name of the group's secret club, and the thing lives in the digital cloud. You liked that you would be subverting the medium, using phones to get people outside and into a real place.

Two sides, one thing. A walk-in sound experience you wander into and choose your own adventure. Secret live shows run from a band account. Separate in the product, woven together underneath.

It belongs to both of you. Next, you tell the group. The season is hope overflowing, the feast, dreaming again, and this plan should feel like that.

## The vision

Five pillars, all from the call.

### Drops and soundscapes

A drop is one piece of audio placed at a spot. It has a radius, say thirty feet, a decibel level at the outer ring and another at the inner ring. Fifty loops in one vibe across a park, and the space is curated. You need no map. You follow your ears.

A soundscape is Israel's layer on top. Each instrument's gain is tied to a location. There is a perfect spot where the mix lines up the way it sounds on the album, in stereo. Walk toward the rock where the violin was played and the violin gets louder. Instruments show as symbols on the map, so you know what you are walking toward. Only soundscapes get this, so they stay special. The live jams and worship sessions are content too.

### Easter eggs

Put a very tight radius on one special object. A stone on top of a hill. People notice it from the path and wonder what that stone is saying. They walk up, and they hear something only there.

Those tight drops are where the quiet things go: Bible verses, prophetic words, life spoken over someone. A ministry Israel was part of used to write hundreds of prophetic words a few times a year. The group could write its own. Nobody is told what the stone is for. The sign says nothing. The stone says it, to whoever climbs the hill.

### Secret shows from a band account

The app generates a QR code for the band. You post it around: flyers, signs, online. Scanning it is the invite. When someone shows up, their phone gets an invitation to join the event locally, with turn-by-turn directions to the radius. A scavenger hunt for music. Live performances only.

Inside the radius during the show, they can enter an app experience with the band. Two ideas came up. Letting the crowd alter the effects, probably a disaster with many people. Or visuals on loop that match the vibe of that area, generated monthly. There is a reward for showing up that nobody else gets, like a recording of that live set. Follow the band and get notified next month. Shows are intimate and rarely repeat.

### Replies and testimonies

A listener can reply to whoever set up the soundscape. Nothing in the app announces itself as religious. So a reply is just a reply. And when someone stands at the perfect spot and feels the presence of God, their testimony comes in the same way. Israel's words: it's all under the cover.

That is why the cover matters. The public app never preaches. The place does the talking, and the curators choose what to say. My gloss: lead with a label and the people you are looking for stay on the path. Under the cover, they walk off it, hear something, and write back.

### Monthly drops and the archive

Once a space is designed, you can drop a new experience into the same park every month. Same paths, same stone, new sound. People keep coming back, and a place becomes a habit. A city might love it because it brings people to the park.

Every secret show adds to the content and to a regional archive. A heat map shows travelers where most of this happens, so someone on a journey knows where to look. The next one is always coming, and if you follow, you are there for it.

## How it feels

Saturday, a little after two. The park in Guthrie is mostly empty. A dog barks somewhere behind the pavilion, and the wind pushes dry leaves across the path in short runs.

There is a sign zip-tied to the post by the trailhead. Small, hand-printed, a square code in the corner. I scan it. The screen shows one line and a ring on a map. I put my earbuds in and the phone in my pocket. I do not look at it again for a while.

At first there is nothing. Just gravel and wind. Then, a few steps on, a pad. Low, slow, one chord that keeps opening. Faint, from behind the trees on the left. I take two steps toward it and it gets a little stronger. Two steps back and it fades. So I leave the path.

The grass is long here and still damp in the shade. I go under a hackberry and the pad is fuller now, and there is something else in it, a voice, far off, singing without words. I walk toward where it feels thickest. Twigs underfoot. The sound pulls me left, then up a slight rise.

And then it lines up. One more step and a violin comes in on the right, clean, and the pad is in the middle, and the voice is behind me and to the left, and I am standing inside a song. It sounds the way a record sounds, except there is a tree in front of me and a plane going over. I stay longer than I mean to.

When I look around, there is a stone at the top of the rise. Flat, grey, a little bigger than a loaf of bread, set there on its own. I walk to it because I want to know what it is saying.

I step up beside it and the music drops back, and a voice I do not know speaks a few slow lines, close, like someone standing next to me. My hand is on the stone. It is warm from the sun.

Then a branch snaps behind me. Someone in a grey hoodie is coming through the same gap in the trees I came through, earbuds in, listening. They stop where the violin comes in, and I watch their face change.

## Things that must stay true

- The public app never preaches. The place does the talking. Curators choose what to say.
- Find the right person. Reach is the wrong measure. No feeds, no public counts, no likes.
- The phone leads you there, then gets out of the way.
- Same park, new experience every month, so a place becomes a habit.
